Orchard calendar

What to do in a home orchard each month, written for a temperate climate (about zones 4–7). Shift it a few weeks earlier in warm areas and later in cold ones.

January

  • Check trunk guards for rodent and rabbit damage.
  • Order new trees and scion wood for spring.
  • Check stored apples; remove any that are rotting.

February

  • Start dormant pruning on mild days: remove dead, crossing and inward-growing branches.
  • Sharpen and disinfect pruning tools.

March

  • Finish pruning before buds break.
  • Apply dormant horticultural oil at "silver tip" to smother overwintering pests.
  • Plant bare-root trees as soon as the soil can be worked.

April

  • Trees bloom — never spray insecticides during bloom; protect the bees.
  • Watch frost forecasts; cover small trees on frosty nights.
  • Apply compost or a light balanced fertilizer around the drip line.

May

  • After petal fall, hang codling moth traps.
  • Begin thinning: leave one apple per cluster, 6–8 in (15–20 cm) apart.
  • Check for scab spots on leaves after wet weather.

June

  • Natural "June drop" happens — finish thinning afterwards.
  • Water young trees about 1 in (2.5 cm) per week in dry spells.
  • Mulch around the base, keeping it away from the trunk.

July

  • Summer-prune water sprouts and vigorous upright shoots.
  • Hang red sticky spheres to trap apple maggot flies.
  • Harvest the earliest varieties.

August

  • Stop all nitrogen feeding so wood hardens before winter.
  • Prop up heavily laden branches.
  • Harvest early varieties; pick up fallen fruit.

September

  • Main harvest begins. Ripe apples lift and twist off easily and have brown seeds.
  • Handle fruit gently — bruises rot in storage.

October

  • Harvest late varieties before hard frost.
  • Rake and remove fallen leaves and fruit to reduce scab next year.
  • Store keepers cool (32–40 °F / 0–4 °C) and humid.

November

  • Install trunk guards and hardware cloth against rodents.
  • Water deeply before the ground freezes if autumn was dry.
  • Paint trunks of young trees with white latex paint to prevent sunscald.

December

  • Trees are dormant — plan next year’s orchard.
  • Check stored fruit regularly.
  • Collect scion wood from favorite trees for grafting.

Orchard basics

Sun

At least 6–8 hours of direct sun. More sun means sweeter fruit and less disease.

Soil

Well-drained loam, pH 6.0–7.0. Avoid low spots where water or frost collects.

Spacing

Roughly the mature height apart: dwarf 6–10 ft, semi-dwarf 12–15 ft, standard 20–30 ft.

Pollination

Almost all apples need a different variety blooming at the same time within about 50–100 ft. Crabapples count!

Planting

Keep the graft union 2–3 in above the soil line, or the variety will root and lose its dwarfing.

First years

Remove blossoms in the first year or two so the tree builds roots and branches first.
