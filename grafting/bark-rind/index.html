Bark (rind) graft

Best for: Thick limbs or trunks of any size. Very forgiving, with high success rates.

WhenSpring, once the bark slips (peels easily)
DifficultyBeginner

Step by step

  1. Wait until the bark lifts easily from the wood, usually a few weeks after bud break.
  2. Cut the limb off squarely.
  3. Make a vertical slit through the bark down from the cut, about 1.5–2 in (4–5 cm) long.
  4. On each scion, make one long, flat cut 1.5 in (4 cm) long, then a short cut on the opposite side to form a point.
  5. Gently lift the bark flaps and slide the scion down behind them, long cut facing the wood.
  6. Hold the scions firmly with tape or a small nail. Space several around a large stub.
  7. Wrap the top of the stub and the bark slits in grafting tape so no cut is left open, and wrap each scion completely, tip and buds included, in stretched grafting tape such as Parafilm. The buds grow through it. Stake or brace the new shoots against wind.

Fruits this works well for

🍎 Apples 🍐 Pears 🍒 Sweet cherries 🟠 Persimmons 🟤 Jujubes 🫒 Olives 🥜 Pecans 🌰 Walnuts 🌰 Black walnuts 🌰 Chestnuts
