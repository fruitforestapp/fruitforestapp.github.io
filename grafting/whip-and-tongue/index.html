Whip and tongue graft

Best for: Rootstock and scion of the same thickness, usually pencil size. The standard bench graft for making new trees.

WhenLate winter–early spring (dormant)
DifficultyBeginner–intermediate

Step by step

  1. Pick a scion and rootstock of matching diameter. Trim the scion to 3–4 buds.
  2. Make one smooth, sloping cut across the top of the rootstock, about 1–1.5 in (3–4 cm) long. Make the same cut at the base of the scion.
  3. On each cut face, start one-third of the way down from the tip and slice back into the wood, parallel to the grain, about ½ in (1 cm). These are the "tongues".
  4. Slide the two pieces together so the tongues interlock. Check that the green cambium lines up on at least one side. If the pieces differ slightly in size, shift the scion to one edge.
  5. Wrap firmly from below the union to above it with grafting tape or Parafilm, so nothing can move.
  6. Wrap the whole scion, tip and buds included, in grafting tape such as Parafilm, stretched thin so it seals in one layer. The buds push through it on their own. Label the graft.
  7. Plant out or pot up, keeping the union above soil level. Keep it cool and out of strong wind until the buds open.
