How to graft fruit trees

A plain-language guide to grafting apples, pears, plums, cherries, citrus and more.

The basics

What grafting does

Most fruit varieties don't grow true from seed: a seed grows into a brand-new, usually ordinary, variety. To get a known variety you join a piece of it (the scion) onto roots (the rootstock). The rootstock decides the plant's size and hardiness. The scion decides the fruit.

The one rule that matters

Line up the cambium. That is the thin green layer just under the bark. It is the only living tissue that can knit together. If the cambium of the scion touches the cambium of the stock on at least one side, and the joint is held tight and kept from drying out, the graft will usually take.

What joins with what

Grafts only take between close relatives: usually the same species, sometimes the same genus. Choose a fruit at the top of this page to see what it joins with, when to graft it and which methods work best.

Dormant or growing

Deciduous fruit trees are usually grafted with dormant scion wood in late winter or spring, and budded in summer. Evergreens such as citrus, avocado, mango and olive are grafted with fresh budwood while they are actively growing in warm weather.

Collecting scion wood

Collect while dormant

Cut scion wood in mid to late winter, after a few hard frosts and before buds swell. Use last season's growth: smooth one-year-old shoots about pencil thickness (6–10 mm), from a healthy, vigorous part of the tree.

Choose leaf buds

Slim, pointed buds are leaf buds. Use those. Fat, round buds are fruit buds and are poor for grafting. Skip the soft tip of the shoot and the crowded buds at its base.

Store cold and damp

Bundle and label each variety. Wrap in a barely damp paper towel inside a sealed plastic bag, and keep at 32–40°F (0–4°C) in a fridge. Keep it away from ripening fruit: the ethylene gas they give off can kill buds. Well-stored wood keeps 2–3 months.

Getting rare varieties

Most of the historic varieties in this app are only available as scion wood. Look for scion exchanges run by local fruit clubs, heritage orchard programs, and national collections. In the US, the USDA collection in Geneva, NY distributes small amounts to researchers; in the UK, the National Fruit Collection at Brogdale does. Always check plant-health rules before moving wood across borders.

Tools and supplies

  • Sharp grafting knife (single-bevel is easiest for flat cuts)
  • Bypass pruners and a fine saw for top-working
  • Grafting tape, rubber budding strips or Parafilm
  • Extra grafting tape for wrapping stubs and other open cuts
  • Plant labels and a waterproof marker
  • Rubbing alcohol to clean blades between trees
  • Cleft tool or a sturdy knife and a mallet (cleft grafts)

Aftercare

First weeks

Buds should swell and open in 2–4 weeks. Leaves that open and then wilt usually mean the scion ran on stored energy without the graft joining.

Remove competition

Rub off every shoot that sprouts from the rootstock below the graft. Left alone, they will outgrow and shade the scion.

Loosen the wrap

Cut rubber strips or tape after 4–8 weeks, before they girdle the union. Parafilm breaks down by itself in sunlight.

Support new growth

Tie the new shoot loosely to a stake or splint. New unions are weak, and wind or a landing bird can snap them.

Go easy

Water steadily but don't feed heavily. Pinch off any flowers in the first year so the tree builds roots and wood instead of fruit.

Family trees

For a multi-variety tree, choose varieties with similar vigor and bloom time, and label every limb. Prune back strong varieties each year so they don't take over.

When grafts fail

Scion turned brown and shriveled

It dried out. Next time, wrap the whole scion and the union completely in grafting tape.

Leafed out, then wilted

The cambium didn't line up or the joint moved. Wrap tighter and check alignment.

Nothing happened at all

The scion wood may have been dead or frozen in storage, or the buds were damaged by ethylene from fruit in the fridge.

Union snapped later

The graft was weak or the shoot was unsupported. Stake new grafts for the first season.
