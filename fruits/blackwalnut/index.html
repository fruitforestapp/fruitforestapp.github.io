🌰 Black walnuts

Juglans nigra

How to grow black walnuts at home: hardiness zones 4–9. Black walnuts set better crops with a second variety or wild trees nearby.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 23 varieties

How to grow black walnuts

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Deep, fertile, well-drained soil.

Spacing

40–60 ft (12–18 m).

Planting

Plant in spring, or sow nuts in autumn where trees will stay.

Care

Roots release juglone, which harms tomatoes, apples and many other plants nearby.

Pruning

Train young trees to a single trunk.

Harvest

Autumn; hull nuts soon after they drop (wear gloves, hulls stain).

First crop

7–10 years.

Common problems

Husk fly, anthracnose, squirrels.

Through the year

Spring

  • Nothing needed.

Summer

  • Water young trees.

Autumn

  • Hull and dry nuts.

Winter

  • Nothing needed.

Grafting black walnuts

Sometimes grafted Black walnuts are usually grown from seed; named nut varieties are grafted onto seedlings.

What it joins with

Black walnut seedlings.

When to graft

Late spring when it is warm; patch budding in summer.

Scion wood

Collect dormant scion wood in winter and keep it cold.

Best methods

  1. Bark (rind): In late spring.
  2. Patch bud: In summer.

Propagating black walnuts

Black walnuts grow easily from seed; named nut varieties are grafted onto seedlings.

From seed

No, but seedlings are good trees.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Seed: Plant fresh nuts in fall, 2 in (5 cm) deep, where the tree will grow; protect them from squirrels.
  2. Bark (rind): In late spring when it is warm.
