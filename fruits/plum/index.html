🟣 Plums

Prunus domestica, P. salicina and hybrids

How to grow plums at home: hardiness zones 3–9. European plums are often self-fertile. Japanese and American hybrid plums usually need a partner of the same group (Toka and native plums pollinate the hybrids).

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 454 varieties

How to grow plums

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Well-drained soil; avoid spots where water stands after rain. pH 6.0–7.5; plums tolerate clay better than most stone fruit.

Spacing

10–20 ft (3–6 m) depending on rootstock.

Planting

Plant in early spring while dormant.

Care

Thin fruit to 3–4 in (8–10 cm) apart so branches don't break. Water during fruit growth.

Pruning

Prune in spring or summer, not winter (silver leaf). European plums: central leader; Japanese plums: open center.

Harvest

Mid summer to early autumn. Ripe plums are slightly soft and come away easily.

First crop

3–5 years.

Common problems

Black knot, brown rot, plum curculio, aphids and silver leaf.

Through the year

Spring

  • Check which group your plum needs as a partner.

Summer

  • Thin fruit; prune after harvest.

Autumn

  • Remove mummified fruit (brown rot).

Winter

  • Cut out black knot swellings.

Grafting plums

Usually grafted Plums are grafted onto plum or peach rootstocks and take fairly well.

What it joins with

Myrobalan, St. Julien, Krymsk, Pixy and other plum rootstocks, peach seedlings, and many other plums. European and Japanese plums can share a tree. Apricots and peaches can often be grafted onto plum as well.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring for whip grafts; summer for budding; spring for top-working.

Scion wood

$_dormantScion $_summerBud

Best methods

  1. Chip bud: In summer; the most reliable.
  2. Whip and tongue: In late winter to early spring.
  3. Cleft: To add varieties to an older tree.
  4. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.

Propagating plums

Plums are budded or grafted onto Myrobalan, St. Julien, Krymsk or peach rootstocks. Plums on their own roots sucker freely.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Chip bud: In summer.
  2. Whip and tongue: In late winter to early spring.
  3. Suckers and division: Only from own-root plums; on grafted trees, suckers are rootstock.
