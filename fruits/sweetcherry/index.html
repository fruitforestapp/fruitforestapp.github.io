🍒 Sweet cherries

Prunus avium

How to grow sweet cherries at home: hardiness zones 5–8. Many sweet cherries need a compatible partner; self-fertile ones (Stella, Lapins, Sweetheart, BlackGold, WhiteGold) can stand alone and pollinate others.

How to grow sweet cherries

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Deep, very well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0; cherries die in wet ground.

Spacing

Gisela 5 trees 8–12 ft (2.5–4 m); standard trees 20–30 ft (6–9 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring on a raised or sloping site.

Care

Net trees against birds as fruit colors. Water evenly; rain on ripe fruit makes it crack.

Pruning

Prune in summer after harvest (not winter) to reduce silver leaf and canker.

Harvest

Early to mid summer; pick with stems when fully colored and sweet.

First crop

3–5 years on Gisela, 5–7 on Mazzard.

Common problems

Birds, cherry fruit fly, brown rot, bacterial canker and spotted wing drosophila.

Through the year

Spring

  • Protect blossom from late frost if you can.

Summer

  • Net against birds; harvest; prune after harvest.

Autumn

  • Clean up fallen leaves.

Winter

  • Check for canker; do not prune now.

Grafting sweet cherries

Usually grafted Sweet cherries are grafted onto cherry rootstocks. Cherries are harder than apples: bud grafts work best.

What it joins with

Mazzard (wild cherry), Mahaleb and Gisela rootstocks, and other sweet or sour cherries. Gisela gives small trees; Mazzard large ones. Cherry onto plum or peach usually fails.

When to graft

Chip or T-budding in mid to late summer is most reliable. Spring grafts work best just as the buds swell, kept warm.

Scion wood

$_dormantScion $_summerBud

Best methods

  1. Chip bud: The most reliable method for cherries.
  2. T-bud (shield bud): In summer while the bark slips.
  3. Whip and tongue: Possible in spring; keep the graft warm, as cherries callus slowly.
  4. Bark (rind): For top-working in late spring.

Tip: Cherries bleed gum from wounds. Cut cleanly, wrap tightly and graft on a dry day.

Propagating sweet cherries

Sweet cherries are budded or grafted onto Mazzard, Mahaleb or Gisela rootstocks.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Chip bud: The most reliable method for cherries, in mid to late summer.
  2. T-bud (shield bud): In summer while the bark slips.
  3. Whip and tongue: Harder with cherries: graft just as the buds swell and keep the graft warm.
