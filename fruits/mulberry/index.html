⚫ Mulberries

Morus alba, M. rubra, M. nigra

How to grow mulberries at home: hardiness zones 4–10. Most mulberries set fruit without a partner.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 14 varieties

How to grow mulberries

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Almost any soil, pH 5.5–7.0.

Spacing

15–30 ft (4.5–9 m); dwarf types 6–10 ft (2–3 m).

Planting

Plant in spring. Keep away from patios and cars; ripe fruit stains.

Care

Very easy and tough. Water young trees in drought.

Pruning

Prune lightly in winter while dormant; mulberries bleed sap if cut in spring.

Harvest

Early to mid summer over several weeks; shake ripe fruit onto a sheet.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Birds; popcorn disease in the southern US.

Through the year

Spring

  • Nothing needed.

Summer

  • Harvest onto a sheet.

Autumn

  • Rake fallen leaves.

Winter

  • Prune while fully dormant.

Grafting mulberries

Sometimes grafted Mulberries root easily from cuttings, but grafting puts a known fruiting variety on vigorous roots.

What it joins with

Mulberry seedlings of the same or related species (white, red and black mulberries often take on each other).

When to graft

Spring as buds swell; summer for budding.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: In early spring.
  2. Cleft: To top-work a seedling mulberry.
  3. Chip bud: In summer.

Propagating mulberries

Mulberries root easily from cuttings.

From seed

No, and seedlings may be male only.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Hardwood cuttings: Take 8–10 in (20–25 cm) cuttings in winter.
  2. Softwood cuttings: Root quickly under mist or a dome.
  3. Air layering: Gives a bigger plant in one season.
