🍎 Belle de Boskoop apple

Boskoop, Netherlands, 1856 · Reinette de Montfort sport

Popular in Germany and the Netherlands for strudel. Triploid; blossoms are frost sensitive.

Triploid. Thought to be a bud sport of Reinette de Montfort. Found by K.J.W. Ottolander, Boskoop, The Netherlands in 1856. Received an Award of Merit from the Royal Horticultural Society in 1897. Fruits have firm, coarse textured flesh with a pleasant, aromatic flavour. Also cooks well.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

Belle de Boskoop (also called Goudrenet, Goudreinet or Goudreinnette) is an apple cultivar which originated in Boskoop, Netherlands, where it began as a chance seedling in 1856. Variants include Boskoop red, yellow and green. This rustic apple is firm, tart and fragrant.

Pollination

Triploid (needs 2 partners). Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time. Triploid apples make poor pollen, so plant two other varieties that can pollinate each other as well as this one.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Anna Antonovka Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Chestnut Crab Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
