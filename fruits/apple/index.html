🍎 Apples

Malus domestica

How to grow apples at home: hardiness zones 3–8. Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time.

How to grow apples

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Well-drained loam, pH 6.0–7.0. Avoid low frost pockets.

Spacing

About the mature width apart: dwarf 6–10 ft (2–3 m), semi-dwarf 12–15 ft (4–5 m), standard 20–25 ft (6–8 m).

Planting

Plant bare-root trees in early spring while dormant. Keep the graft union 2–3 in (5–8 cm) above the soil.

Care

Water weekly in the first two summers. Mulch a wide ring but keep it off the trunk. Thin fruit to one per cluster.

Pruning

Prune in late winter. Train to a central leader; remove dead, crossing and inward branches.

Harvest

Late summer to late autumn, by variety. Ripe apples lift and twist off easily and the seeds turn brown.

First crop

2–4 years on dwarf rootstocks, 5–8 on standard trees.

Common problems

Apple scab, fire blight, cedar apple rust, codling moth and apple maggot. Disease-resistant varieties save a lot of spraying.

Through the year

Spring

  • Plant new trees.
  • Spray dormant oil before buds open if scale or mites were a problem.

Summer

  • Thin fruit to one per cluster by early summer.
  • Hang traps for codling moth and apple maggot.

Autumn

  • Harvest and store fruit.
  • Rake up fallen leaves and fruit to reduce scab.

Winter

  • Prune on mild days.
  • Check trunk guards for rodent damage.

Grafting apples

Usually grafted Almost every apple tree is grafted. The variety is joined onto a rootstock, which sets the tree's size and hardiness.

What it joins with

Any apple rootstock, apple seedlings and crabapples. Many varieties can share one tree. Apple onto pear, quince or hawthorn usually fails within a few years.

When to graft

Bench grafts in late winter to early spring while dormant. Top-working in spring from bud break until the bark slips. Budding in mid to late summer.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: The standard way to make new trees on bare-root rootstocks.
  2. Cleft: To change the variety of a branch or young tree.
  3. Chip bud: In summer, or in spring; very reliable on apples.
  4. Bark (rind): For thick limbs once the bark slips.
  5. T-bud (shield bud): Nursery budding in summer.

Propagating apples

Apples are grown by grafting a piece of the variety you want onto a rootstock. Cuttings rarely root.

From seed

No. Every apple seed grows into a new variety.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto bare-root apple rootstocks in late winter.
  2. Chip bud: In summer onto young rootstocks.
  3. Cleft: To change the variety on an established tree.
