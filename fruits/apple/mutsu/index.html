🍎 Mutsu apple

Aomori, Japan, 1948 · Golden Delicious × Indo

Huge, juicy apples. Triploid — plant two other varieties.

Triploid. Raised in Japan in 1930 and first fruited in 1939. It was named 'Mutsu' in 1948 but renamed 'Crispin' in the UK for commercial reasons in 1968. Fruits have firm, fine-textured, juicy flesh which is a little sweet, somewhat acid with a refreshing, and pleasant flavour.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

The Mutsu (陸奥, ムツ) apple (also known as Crispin) was introduced in 1949 and is a cross between the 'Golden Delicious' and the 'Indo' apple cultivars first grown in Aomori Prefecture, Japan. The apple's name is the former name of a large section of the Tōhoku region, Mutsu Province, which Aomori was created from during the Meiji Restoration. 'Mutsu' is a triploid cultivar.

Pollination

Triploid (needs 2 partners). Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time. Triploid apples make poor pollen, so plant two other varieties that can pollinate each other as well as this one.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Antonovka Arkansas Black Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin Dabinett

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
