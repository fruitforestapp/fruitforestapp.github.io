🍎 Gravenstein apple

Denmark / northern Germany, 1600s · Unknown

Superb applesauce apple. Triploid — needs two pollinating partners.

Triploid. Said to have originated either in the garden of the Duke of Augustenberg, Castle of Graefenstein, Schleswig-Holstein or in Italy or Southern Tyrol and sent to Schleswig-Holstein, or scions from Italy sent home by a brother of Count Chr. Ahlefeldt of Graasten Castle, South Jutland. It is thought to have arrived in Denmark in about 1669. Fruits have crisp, rather coarse-textured, juicy flesh with a pleasant mixture of sweetness and acidity with a distinctive flavour.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

Gravenstein (Danish: Gråsten, meaning "graystone", after Gråsten Palace) is a triploid apple cultivar that originated in the 17th century or earlier. The fruit has a tart flavor, and it is heavily used as a cooking apple, especially for apple sauce and apple cider. It does not keep well, and it is available only in season.

Pollination

Triploid (needs 2 partners). Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time. Triploid apples make poor pollen, so plant two other varieties that can pollinate each other as well as this one.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Anna Antonovka Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Chestnut Crab Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
