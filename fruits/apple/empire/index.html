🍎 Empire apple

Geneva, New York, USA, 1945 (released 1966) · McIntosh × Red Delicious

Fruit hangs well on the tree and resists bruising.

Raised in 1945 at New York State Agricultural Experiment Station, Geneva, New York. It was named and introduced in 1966. Fruits have crisp flesh with a subacid and aromatic flavour.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

Empire is a clonally propagated cultivar of apple derived from a seed sown in 1945 by Lester C. Anderson, a Cornell University fruit nutritionist who conducted open pollination research on his various orchards. Pomologist and apple breeder Roger Way named this new variety the Empire after its home state, New York (the "Empire State"). This variety is mostly grown in the Northeastern United States.

Pollination

Needs a partner. Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Antonovka Arkansas Black Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Chestnut Crab Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
