🍎 Jonathan apple

Woodstock, New York, USA, c. 1826 · Seedling of Esopus Spitzenburg

Classic all-purpose heirloom. Avoid if cedar trees (junipers) grow nearby.

Originated on the farm of Philip Rick, Woodstock, Ulster County, New York, USA. It was first described in 1826 by Judge Buel who named the apple after Jonathan Hasbrouck. Fruits have soft, fine-textured, fairly juicy flesh with a sweet and fairly pleasant flavour.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

Jonathan is a cultivated variety or cultivar of apple, thought to have originated from the town of Woodstock, New York, United States in the nineteenth century. It has been used to breed many new cultivars.

Pollination

Needs a partner. Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Antonovka Arkansas Black Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Chestnut Crab Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
