🍎 Beauty of Kent

Apple · Malus domestica

Beauty of Kent is an apple variety from England, first recorded c. 1820.

Thought to be of English origin. Introduced in about 1820. Received an Award of Merit from the Royal Horticultural Society in 1901. Fruits are coarse textured, juicy and subacid, with a faint pleasant flavour.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

'Beauty of Kent' is a cultivar of apples; the fruit are used for cooking. It is known by various names including 'Countess of Warwick', 'Gadd's Seedling', and 'Wooling's Favourite'. It received an Award of Merit from the Royal Horticultural Society in 1901 but was judged of no value for the apple-growing areas of New York State in 1913.
