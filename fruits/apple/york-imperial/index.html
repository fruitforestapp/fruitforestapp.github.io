🍎 York Imperial apple

York, Pennsylvania, USA, c. 1820 · Unknown

Still used commercially for applesauce. Its lopsided shape makes it easy to recognize.

The 'York Imperial', or 'York', is a cultivar of apple (Malus pumila) from which a number of other valuable strains and cultivars have arisen, including four sport varieties: Commander York, Ramey York, Red Yorking, and Yorking. Most York apples are sold to food processors to be turned into pantry ingredients, such as sauces, cider, and vinegar, rather than sold directly to consumers. Pennsylvania is the top producer of York apples, with 2.7 million out of the 3.7 million bushels produced in 2025 coming from the state; the rest are primarily grown in Virginia and New York.

Pollination

Needs a partner. Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Antonovka Arkansas Black Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Chestnut Crab Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
