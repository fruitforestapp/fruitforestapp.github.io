🍎 Macoun apple

Geneva, New York, USA, 1923 · McIntosh × Jersey Black

A New England favorite. Fruit drops easily — pick promptly.

Raised at the New York State Agricultural Experiment Station, Geneva, USA by Richard Wellington. It was first described in 1918. Introduced in 1923. Fruits have crisp, tender, white flesh with a sweet, scented flavour.

Description from the National Fruit Collection, Brogdale (Open Government Licence).

The Macoun (pronounced Ma-cown, Ma-coon or McCowan) is a cultivated variety of apples developed at the New York State Agricultural Experiment Station in Geneva, New York. Named after Canadian horticulturalist W. T. Macoun, it is a hybrid the McIntosh and Jersey Black. Cross pollination was done in 1909 and the Macoun was commercially released in 1932.

Pollination

Needs a partner. Apples need a second apple or crabapple variety that blooms at the same time.

Varieties in this library that bloom at about the same time:

Akane Ambrosia Antonovka Arkansas Black Ashmead's Kernel Braeburn Calville Blanc d'Hiver Cameo Chestnut Crab Cortland Cosmic Crisp Cox's Orange Pippin

Bloom times overlap differently from year to year and place to place. A nearby crabapple is a good backup pollinator.
