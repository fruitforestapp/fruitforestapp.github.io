🟢 Pistachios

Pistacia vera

How to grow pistachios at home: hardiness zones 7–10. Pistachio trees are male or female; plant a male (Peters) near female trees.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 43 varieties

How to grow pistachios

Sun

Full sun; long hot dry summers and cool winters.

Soil

Deep, well-drained soil; tolerates alkaline soil.

Spacing

20–25 ft (6–7.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring; one male for up to 8–10 females.

Care

Drought tolerant once established; water deeply in summer.

Pruning

Train to an open vase while young.

Harvest

Late summer to early autumn, when hulls turn rosy and shells split.

First crop

5–8 years.

Common problems

Wet soil, botryosphaeria blight, alternate bearing.

Through the year

Spring

  • Plant a male tree nearby.

Summer

  • Water deeply.

Autumn

  • Harvest and dry quickly.

Winter

  • Needs winter chill.

Rootstocks for pistachios

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted pistachio tree. 5 options:

Grafting pistachios

Usually grafted Pistachios are budded onto other Pistacia species. Plant a male tree nearby.

What it joins with

Pistacia atlantica, P. integerrima, UCB-1 hybrids and P. terebinthus.

When to graft

Budding in late spring to summer.

Scion wood

For summer budding, cut budsticks of this season's growth on the day you bud, clip off the leaves and keep them damp and shaded.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
  3. Patch bud: Also used on pistachios.

Propagating pistachios

Pistachios are budded onto Pistacia atlantica, P. integerrima or UCB-1 rootstocks. You need a male tree nearby.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
