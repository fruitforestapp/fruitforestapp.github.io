🌰 Walnuts

Juglans regia

How to grow walnuts at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Walnuts are self-fertile but bloom timing often mismatches; a second variety helps.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 125 varieties

How to grow walnuts

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Deep, well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.5.

Spacing

30–50 ft (9–15 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring; avoid late-frost spots.

Care

Water deeply in summer. Keep away from tomatoes and other juglone-sensitive plants.

Pruning

Prune in late summer (cuts bleed in spring).

Harvest

Autumn, when the green hulls split and nuts drop.

First crop

4–7 years.

Common problems

Walnut blight, husk fly, codling moth, late frost.

Through the year

Spring

  • Watch for late frost.

Summer

  • Water; prune late summer.

Autumn

  • Gather and dry nuts.

Winter

  • Nothing needed.

Grafting walnuts

Usually grafted Walnuts are grafted onto walnut seedlings, but grafts need warmth, around 80°F (27°C), to knit.

What it joins with

English walnut seedlings, black walnut, Paradox hybrids and other walnuts.

When to graft

Late spring when it is hot; patch budding in summer.

Scion wood

Collect dormant scion wood in winter and keep it cold until the weather is warm.

Best methods

  1. Bark (rind): In late spring.
  2. Patch bud: In summer.
  3. Whip and tongue: Bench grafts kept in a warm callusing box.

Tip: Walnuts bleed sap in spring. Cut the stock a few days before grafting, or make a small cut lower down to let the sap drain.

Propagating walnuts

Walnuts are grafted onto seedlings, but grafts need real warmth, around 80°F (27°C), to knit.

From seed

No, but seedlings are vigorous.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Bark (rind): In late spring when the leaves are opening and the weather is hot.
  2. Seed: Plant fresh nuts in fall, 2 in (5 cm) deep; protect them from squirrels.
