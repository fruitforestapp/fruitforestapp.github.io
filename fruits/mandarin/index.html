🍊 Mandarins

Citrus reticulata

How to grow mandarins at home: hardiness zones 8–11. Most mandarins are self-fertile; some hybrids set more fruit with a pollinizer.

How to grow mandarins

Sun

Full sun.

Soil

Very well-drained, slightly acid soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

10–20 ft (3–6 m); great in pots.

Planting

Plant in spring. Satsumas are among the most cold-tolerant citrus.

Care

Feed with citrus fertilizer 3–4 times a year; water deeply.

Pruning

Minimal; remove suckers and dead wood. Thin crop on heavy years to avoid alternate bearing.

Harvest

Autumn to winter; fruit holds a short time on the tree once ripe.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Frost, scale, aphids, leaf miner.

Through the year

Spring

  • Feed.

Summer

  • Water.

Autumn

  • Move pots indoors before frost.

Winter

  • Harvest; protect from frost.

Grafting mandarins

Usually grafted Mandarins are budded onto citrus rootstocks, like oranges.

What it joins with

Trifoliate orange, citrange, sour orange, citrumelo and other citrus.

When to graft

Whenever the bark slips in warm weather.

Scion wood

Citrus is evergreen: use budwood, not dormant sticks. Cut rounded, recently hardened shoots with plump buds, clip the leaves to short stubs and use them within a few days.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The standard citrus method.
  2. Chip bud: In warm weather.
  3. Side veneer: On small potted stocks.

Propagating mandarins

Mandarins are budded onto citrus rootstocks, like oranges.

From seed

Often yes for many types, but slow to fruit.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In warm weather while the bark slips.
  2. Air layering: In spring or summer.
