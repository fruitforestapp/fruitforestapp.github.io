🔴 Pomegranates

Punica granatum

How to grow pomegranates at home: hardiness zones 7–10. Pomegranates are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 221 varieties

How to grow pomegranates

Sun

Full sun and hot summers.

Soil

Most well-drained soils, pH 5.5–7.2; tolerates some salt.

Spacing

12–18 ft (3.5–5.5 m); bushes or small trees.

Planting

Plant in spring after frost.

Care

Water deeply and regularly; uneven watering splits fruit.

Pruning

Prune in late winter; keep 3–5 main trunks and remove suckers.

Harvest

Autumn, when fruit sounds metallic when tapped and color is full.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Fruit splitting, leaf-footed bugs, cold below about 10 °F (−12 °C).

Through the year

Spring

  • Prune.

Summer

  • Water deeply.

Autumn

  • Harvest before heavy rain.

Winter

  • Protect in cold snaps.

Rootstocks for pomegranates

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted pomegranate tree. 3 options:

RootstockSizeZones
Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit
Pomegranate seedlingVigorousAs the fruit
Another pomegranate variety (top-working)As the fruit

Grafting pomegranates

Rarely grafted Pomegranates root easily, so they are rarely grafted. Grafting can change the variety of an established bush.

What it joins with

Other pomegranates.

When to graft

Spring.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Cleft: In spring.
  2. Whip and tongue: In spring.

Propagating pomegranates

Pomegranates root easily from cuttings.

From seed

No, but seedlings grow fast.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Hardwood cuttings: Take 10 in (25 cm) cuttings in winter.
  2. Suckers and division: Pomegranates sucker freely from the base.
  3. Air layering: In spring or summer.
