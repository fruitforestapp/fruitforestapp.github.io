🟤 Medlars

Mespilus germanica

How to grow medlars at home: hardiness zones 5–8. Medlars are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 30 varieties

How to grow medlars

Sun

Full sun to light shade.

Soil

Most well-drained soils, pH 6.0–7.5.

Spacing

15–20 ft (4.5–6 m).

Planting

Plant while dormant.

Care

Easy care. Fruit is eaten after it softens ("blets") in storage.

Pruning

Prune lightly in winter for shape.

Harvest

Late autumn after the first frost; store until soft and brown inside.

First crop

3–4 years.

Common problems

Few problems.

Through the year

Spring

  • Nothing needed.

Summer

  • Water young trees.

Autumn

  • Pick late; blet indoors 2–3 weeks.

Winter

  • Light pruning.

Grafting medlars

Usually grafted Medlars are almost always grafted, because seed is slow and variable.

What it joins with

Quince, hawthorn, pear, and medlar seedlings. Quince gives a smaller tree; hawthorn a hardy one for heavy soil.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring; summer for budding.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto quince or hawthorn in late winter.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
  3. Cleft: To top-work a hawthorn.

Propagating medlars

Medlars are grafted onto hawthorn, quince or pear.

From seed

No, and the seed can take two winters to sprout.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Whip and tongue: In late winter onto quince, hawthorn or pear stock.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
