🫐 Haskaps

Lonicera caerulea

How to grow haskaps at home: hardiness zones 2–7. Haskaps need a second, compatible variety that blooms at the same time.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 37 varieties

How to grow haskaps

Sun

Full sun to part shade.

Soil

Most soils, pH 5.5–7.5.

Spacing

4–5 ft (1.2–1.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring or autumn; extremely cold hardy.

Care

Net against birds. Water in dry spells.

Pruning

Little until year 5; then remove the oldest stems.

Harvest

Late spring to early summer, earlier than strawberries; berries are ripe when purple inside.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Birds.

Through the year

Spring

  • Flowers survive hard frosts.

Summer

  • Net; harvest.

Autumn

  • Nothing needed.

Winter

  • Nothing needed.

Grafting haskaps

Rarely grafted Haskaps grow easily from cuttings and are almost never grafted.

What it joins with

Other honeysuckles (Lonicera), rarely.

When to graft

Late winter.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Propagating haskaps

Haskaps (honeyberries) root well from cuttings. Plant two compatible varieties for fruit.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Softwood cuttings: Early summer, under a dome; roots in 3–5 weeks.
  2. Hardwood cuttings: In late winter.
