🥭 Pawpaws

Asimina triloba

How to grow pawpaws at home: hardiness zones 5–8. Pawpaws need two genetically different trees (two named varieties or seedlings) to set fruit.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 18 varieties

How to grow pawpaws

Sun

Light shade for the first 2 years, then full sun for best crops.

Soil

Rich, moist, well-drained soil, pH 5.5–7.0.

Spacing

8–15 ft (2.5–4.5 m); plant in groups.

Planting

Plant small potted trees in spring; long taproot dislikes moving. Shade young trees.

Care

Water regularly. Hand-pollinating flowers with a small brush improves fruit set.

Pruning

Little needed; remove dead wood and root suckers.

Harvest

Late summer to early autumn when fruit gives like a ripe peach and smells fruity.

First crop

4–7 years.

Common problems

Few pests. Poor pollination is the usual reason for no fruit.

Through the year

Spring

  • Hand-pollinate between two different trees.

Summer

  • Water in dry spells.

Autumn

  • Pick soft fruit; eat within days.

Winter

  • Nothing needed.

Rootstocks for pawpaws

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted pawpaw tree. 2 options:

RootstockSizeZones
Pawpaw seedling (Asimina triloba)VigorousAs the fruit
Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit

Grafting pawpaws

Usually grafted Named pawpaw varieties are grafted onto pawpaw seedlings. Pawpaws graft best when it is warm.

What it joins with

Pawpaw seedlings only.

When to graft

Spring, once the rootstock's buds are opening and days are warm.

Scion wood

Collect dormant scion wood in late winter and keep it cold until the stock leafs out.

Best methods

  1. Cleft: The most common method, on seedlings in spring.
  2. Whip and tongue: On small seedlings of matching size.
  3. Chip bud: In spring or summer.

Propagating pawpaws

Pawpaws grow from seed or are grafted onto seedlings. Root suckers rarely survive being moved.

From seed

No, but seedlings often have good fruit. Never let the seed dry out; give it 90–120 days cold.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Seed: Keep the seed moist from the fruit on. Sow in deep pots for the long taproot and shade seedlings in their first year.
  2. Cleft: Onto seedlings in spring once the stock's buds are opening and the weather is warm.
  3. Chip bud: In summer.
