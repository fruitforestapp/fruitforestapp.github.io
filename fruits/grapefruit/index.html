🍊 Grapefruit

Citrus × paradisi

How to grow grapefruit at home: hardiness zones 9–11. Grapefruit are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 6 varieties

How to grow grapefruit

Sun

Full sun and long hot summers.

Soil

Very well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

15–25 ft (4.5–7.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring.

Care

Feed with citrus fertilizer; water deeply. Needs heat to sweeten.

Pruning

Minimal; remove suckers and dead wood.

Harvest

Winter to spring; fruit stores well on the tree.

First crop

3–4 years.

Common problems

Frost, scale, leaf miner.

Through the year

Spring

  • Feed.

Summer

  • Water.

Autumn

  • Nothing special.

Winter

  • Harvest; frost protection.

Grafting grapefruit

Usually grafted Grapefruit is budded onto citrus rootstocks.

What it joins with

Sour orange, citrumelo, trifoliate orange, citrange and other citrus.

When to graft

Whenever the bark slips in warm weather.

Scion wood

Citrus is evergreen: use budwood, not dormant sticks. Cut rounded, recently hardened shoots with plump buds, clip the leaves to short stubs and use them within a few days.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The standard citrus method.
  2. Chip bud: In warm weather.

Propagating grapefruit

Grapefruit is budded onto citrus rootstocks.

From seed

Usually yes, but seedlings take many years to fruit.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In warm weather while the bark slips.
  2. Air layering: In spring or summer.
