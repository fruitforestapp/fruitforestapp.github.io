🍓 Raspberries

Rubus idaeus, R. occidentalis

How to grow raspberries at home: hardiness zones 3–9. Raspberries are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 215 varieties

How to grow raspberries

Sun

Full sun.

Soil

Rich, well-drained soil, pH 5.5–6.5.

Spacing

2–3 ft (60–90 cm) apart in rows 6–8 ft (2–2.5 m) apart. Use a trellis.

Planting

Plant in early spring; cut new canes back to 6 in (15 cm).

Care

Water 1 in (2.5 cm) a week. Mulch. Keep the row narrow.

Pruning

Summer-bearers: cut fruited canes to the ground after harvest. Fall-bearers: mow all canes in late winter.

Harvest

Summer (summer-bearing) or late summer to frost (fall-bearing).

First crop

1–2 years.

Common problems

Cane diseases, Japanese beetles, spotted wing drosophila, root rot in wet soil.

Through the year

Spring

  • Thin canes to 4–6 per foot of row.

Summer

  • Harvest; cut out fruited canes.

Autumn

  • Harvest fall-bearers.

Winter

  • Mow fall-bearing canes.

Grafting raspberries

Rarely grafted Raspberries aren't grafted. They are grown from suckers, tip layers and root cuttings.

What it joins with

Not grafted.

When to graft

Not grafted.

Scion wood

Not needed.

Propagating raspberries

Red raspberries spread by their roots; black and purple raspberries root at their cane tips.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Suckers and division: For red and yellow raspberries: dig new canes with roots.
  2. Tip layering: For black and purple raspberries, in late summer.
  3. Root cuttings: For red raspberries; a good way to get clean, new plants.

Tip: Only take new plants from healthy patches. Viruses spread with the plants.
