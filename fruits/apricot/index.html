🟠 Apricots

Prunus armeniaca

How to grow apricots at home: hardiness zones 4–8. Most apricots are self-fertile; a few (Moongold, Sungold, Harcot in some areas) need a partner.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 767 varieties

How to grow apricots

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Deep, well-drained soil, pH 6.5–8.0.

Spacing

15–20 ft (4.5–6 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring where late frosts are unlikely, or on a north slope to delay bloom.

Care

Apricots bloom very early; frost often takes the crop. Thin fruit to 2–3 in (5–8 cm) apart.

Pruning

Prune in late summer after harvest to reduce canker; open center or modified leader.

Harvest

Early to mid summer when fully colored and slightly soft.

First crop

3–4 years.

Common problems

Spring frost, brown rot, bacterial canker, plum curculio.

Through the year

Spring

  • Cover small trees on frosty nights in bloom.

Summer

  • Thin; harvest; prune after harvest.

Autumn

  • Clean up fallen fruit.

Winter

  • Choose late-blooming varieties if frost is a problem.

Grafting apricots

Usually grafted Apricots are budded onto apricot, peach or plum stocks.

What it joins with

Apricot and peach seedlings, and plum rootstocks such as St. Julien or Myrobalan for wetter soils.

When to graft

Budding in mid to late summer; whip grafts in early spring.

Scion wood

$_dormantScion $_summerBud

Best methods

  1. Chip bud: In summer.
  2. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  3. Whip and tongue: In early spring.

Propagating apricots

Apricots are budded onto apricot or peach seedlings, or onto some plum rootstocks.

From seed

Somewhat. Hardy seedling apricots are common, but they vary. Seed needs 60–90 days cold.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Chip bud: In summer.
  2. T-bud (shield bud): In summer while the bark slips.
  3. Seed: For rootstocks or to try your luck: 60–90 days cold.
