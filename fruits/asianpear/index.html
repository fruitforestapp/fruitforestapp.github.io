🍐 Asian pears

Pyrus pyrifolia

How to grow asian pears at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Asian pears need a second Asian (or early European) pear for pollination.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 167 varieties

How to grow asian pears

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Well-drained soil; avoid spots where water stands after rain. pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

12–20 ft (4–6 m) apart depending on rootstock.

Planting

Plant while dormant in early spring.

Care

Thin hard to one fruit per cluster, 6 in (15 cm) apart, for good size. Water regularly while fruit grows.

Pruning

Prune in late winter; open center or central leader. They bear on spurs like apples.

Harvest

Ripen on the tree: pick when they taste sweet and crisp and the skin color changes.

First crop

3–4 years.

Common problems

Fire blight and pear psylla; birds and wasps love the sweet fruit.

Through the year

Spring

  • Plant a second Asian or early European pear for pollen.

Summer

  • Thin heavily.

Autumn

  • Pick ripe from the tree.

Winter

  • Prune.

Grafting asian pears

Usually grafted Asian pears graft like European pears. They can go on the same tree as European pears.

What it joins with

Pyrus betulifolia, Callery pear, European pear rootstocks (OHxF) and other pears. Quince is a poor match for most Asian pears.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring; summer for budding.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: In late winter.
  2. Cleft: To add varieties to an existing pear.
  3. Chip bud: In summer.

Propagating asian pears

Asian pears are grafted onto Pyrus betulifolia, Callery pear or European pear rootstocks.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Whip and tongue: In late winter while dormant.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
  3. Cleft: To add varieties to an existing tree.
