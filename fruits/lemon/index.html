🍋 Lemons

Citrus × limon

How to grow lemons at home: hardiness zones 9–11. Lemons are self-fertile.

How to grow lemons

Sun

Full sun; warm sheltered spot.

Soil

Very well-drained soil, pH 5.5–6.5.

Spacing

10–20 ft (3–6 m); Meyer lemons do well in pots.

Planting

Plant in spring. Lemons are less cold-hardy than oranges.

Care

Feed regularly; water when the top inch is dry.

Pruning

Prune lightly to shape and remove suckers.

Harvest

Much of the year; pick when fully colored and slightly soft.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Frost, scale, mealybugs and leaf miner.

Through the year

Spring

  • Feed.

Summer

  • Water.

Autumn

  • Bring pots indoors before frost.

Winter

  • Protect from frost.

Grafting lemons

Sometimes grafted Lemons root well, but grafting onto a rootstock gives a tougher, more disease-resistant tree.

What it joins with

Trifoliate orange, citrange, Volkameriana, rough lemon, sour orange and other citrus.

When to graft

Whenever the bark slips in warm weather.

Scion wood

Citrus is evergreen: use budwood, not dormant sticks. Cut rounded, recently hardened shoots with plump buds, clip the leaves to short stubs and use them within a few days.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The standard citrus method.
  2. Chip bud: In warm weather.

Propagating lemons

Lemons root well from cuttings, so they can grow on their own roots.

From seed

Not reliably.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Semi-hardwood cuttings: Take 4–6 in (10–15 cm) cuttings with rooting hormone and keep them warm and humid.
  2. Air layering: Fast and reliable.
  3. T-bud (shield bud): Onto citrus rootstock for a tougher tree.
