🍑 Peaches

Prunus persica

How to grow peaches at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Nearly all peaches are self-fertile; one tree is enough.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 1,271 varieties

How to grow peaches

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Light, very well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0. Plant on a slope to escape spring frost.

Spacing

15–20 ft (4.5–6 m); genetic dwarfs 6–8 ft (2–2.5 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring. Choose varieties with enough chill for your area.

Care

Thin fruit to 6–8 in (15–20 cm) apart. Feed in spring. Water in dry summers.

Pruning

Prune every year in late winter to early spring, to an open vase; peaches fruit on last year's wood.

Harvest

Mid to late summer, when the green background color turns yellow and the fruit gives slightly.

First crop

2–4 years.

Common problems

Peach leaf curl, brown rot, oriental fruit moth, borers and bacterial spot.

Through the year

Spring

  • Prune to an open vase; protect blossom from frost.

Summer

  • Thin fruit; harvest.

Autumn

  • Clean up mummies; spray for leaf curl after leaf fall.

Winter

  • Spray copper or lime sulfur for leaf curl before buds swell.

Grafting peaches

Usually grafted Peaches are budded in summer. Spring grafts on peaches often fail.

What it joins with

Peach seedlings (Lovell, Halford, Bailey), peach-almond hybrids and some plum rootstocks. Peach, nectarine, almond and apricot can often share a tree.

When to graft

T- or chip budding in mid to late summer while the bark slips.

Scion wood

For summer budding, cut budsticks of this season's growth on the day you bud, clip off the leaves and keep them damp and shaded.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The classic method for peaches.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
  3. Whip and tongue: Possible in early spring, but less reliable on peaches.

Propagating peaches

Peaches are budded in summer onto peach seedlings (such as Lovell or Halford) or plum rootstocks.

From seed

Often fairly close: peaches pollinate themselves, so seedlings tend to resemble the parent. Seed needs about 90–120 days cold.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The classic method, in summer while the bark slips.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
  3. Seed: Crack open the stone or give it 90–120 days cold, and sow in spring.
