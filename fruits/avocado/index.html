🥑 Avocados

Persea americana

How to grow avocados at home: hardiness zones 9–11. Avocados flower as type A or B. A lone tree crops, but planting one of each type raises yields.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 303 varieties

How to grow avocados

Sun

Full sun; shelter from wind.

Soil

Very well-drained soil, pH 6.0–6.5; avocados die in wet soil (root rot).

Spacing

20–30 ft (6–9 m); dwarf varieties 8–12 ft (2.5–3.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring on a mound. Don't disturb the roots.

Care

Water deeply but infrequently. Keep leaf litter under the tree as mulch.

Pruning

Prune lightly to control height after harvest.

Harvest

Fruit doesn't soften on the tree; pick mature fruit and ripen indoors.

First crop

3–4 years from grafted trees.

Common problems

Phytophthora root rot, frost and sunburn.

Through the year

Spring

  • Plant an A and a B type for better crops.

Summer

  • Water deeply.

Autumn

  • Pick and ripen indoors.

Winter

  • Protect from frost.

Grafting avocados

Usually grafted Named avocados are grafted onto seedlings; it is the only way to get a known variety.

What it joins with

Avocado seedlings and avocado rootstocks such as Duke 7 or Dusa.

When to graft

Spring to early summer in warm weather, when the stock is actively growing.

Scion wood

This is an evergreen, so there is no dormant wood. Cut firm, recently matured tip shoots with plump buds, trim the leaves to stubs and graft within a day or two.

Best methods

  1. Cleft: Onto a pencil-thick seedling; the most common home method.
  2. Side veneer: Keeps the seedling's top growing until the graft takes.
  3. Whip and tongue: On seedlings of matching size.

Tip: Choose scions with a fat, swollen tip bud, and keep the grafted plant warm and lightly shaded.

Propagating avocados

A pit sprouts easily, but the tree takes years to fruit and won't match the parent. For a known variety, graft the seedling.

From seed

No, and seedlings may take 5–13 years to fruit.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Seed: Sow the fresh pit, pointed end up, with the top just showing. No cold needed.
  2. Cleft: Graft a named variety onto the seedling once its stem is pencil thick, in warm weather.
