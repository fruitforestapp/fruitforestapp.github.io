🟢 Gooseberries

Ribes uva-crispa, R. hirtellum

How to grow gooseberries at home: hardiness zones 3–7. Gooseberries are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 203 varieties

How to grow gooseberries

Sun

Full sun to part shade.

Soil

Moist, well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

3–5 ft (1–1.5 m).

Planting

Plant in autumn or early spring.

Care

Mulch and water. Thorny: wear gloves.

Pruning

Winter: open the center and remove stems over 3 years old.

Harvest

Early summer (green for cooking) to mid summer (ripe and sweet).

First crop

1–2 years.

Common problems

American gooseberry mildew, sawfly.

Through the year

Spring

  • Watch for sawfly.

Summer

  • Harvest.

Autumn

  • Mulch.

Winter

  • Prune to open the center.

Rootstocks for gooseberries

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted gooseberry tree. 2 options:

RootstockSizeZones
Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit
Golden currant (Ribes aureum), for tree formVigorous3–7

Grafting gooseberries

Rarely grafted Gooseberries are usually layered, but grafting onto golden currant makes a small standard tree that is easy to pick.

What it joins with

Golden currant (Ribes aureum) and other Ribes.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto a golden currant stem for a standard.
  2. Cleft: Onto a golden currant stem.

Propagating gooseberries

Gooseberries root from cuttings or, more reliably, by layering.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Mound (stool) layering: The most reliable way.
  2. Hardwood cuttings: In early fall; rub off the lower buds so the bush grows on a short leg.
  3. Simple layering: Low branches root where they touch the soil.
