🥜 Pecans

Carya illinoinensis

How to grow pecans at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Pecans shed pollen and accept it at different times (type I or II); plant one of each type for good crops.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 363 varieties

How to grow pecans

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Deep, well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

35–60 ft (10–18 m); big trees.

Planting

Plant in late winter or early spring. Keep the long taproot intact.

Care

Water deeply in dry summers. Pecans need zinc; a zinc spray helps on alkaline soils.

Pruning

Train to a central leader while young.

Harvest

Autumn, when the husks split.

First crop

6–10 years.

Common problems

Pecan scab, weevils, squirrels.

Through the year

Spring

  • Plant a type I and type II variety together.

Summer

  • Water deeply.

Autumn

  • Harvest nuts; beat squirrels to them.

Winter

  • Prune young trees.

Grafting pecans

Usually grafted Named pecans are grafted onto pecan seedlings. Pecans need real heat for grafts to knit.

What it joins with

Pecan seedlings, and other hickories with less success.

When to graft

Spring, once the stock is leafing out and days are warm; patch budding in summer.

Scion wood

Collect dormant scion wood in winter, keep it cold, and graft when the stock is leafing out.

Best methods

  1. Bark (rind): Very successful on pecans; the related four-flap graft is popular too.
  2. Patch bud: In summer.
  3. Whip and tongue: On small seedlings in spring.

Propagating pecans

Named pecans are grafted onto pecan seedlings. Pecans need heat for grafts to knit.

From seed

No. Seed needs about 60–90 days cold.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Bark (rind): In spring once the stock is leafing out and days are warm.
  2. Whip and tongue: On small seedlings in spring.
  3. Seed: For rootstocks: 60–90 days cold.

Tip: A popular method for pecans is the four-flap graft. It is close to a bark graft and done at the same time.
