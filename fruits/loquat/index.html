🟠 Loquats

Eriobotrya japonica

How to grow loquats at home: hardiness zones 8–10. Many loquats set fruit alone; some need a partner.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 77 varieties

How to grow loquats

Sun

Full sun to light shade.

Soil

Well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.5.

Spacing

15–20 ft (4.5–6 m).

Planting

Plant in spring.

Care

Easy care. Thin fruit clusters for bigger fruit.

Pruning

Prune after harvest to keep the tree low.

Harvest

Late winter to spring, when fruit is fully colored and slightly soft.

First crop

2–3 years from grafted trees.

Common problems

Fire blight; blossom and fruit lost to frost (it flowers in autumn/winter).

Through the year

Spring

  • Harvest.

Summer

  • Prune after harvest.

Autumn

  • Flowers open.

Winter

  • Protect flowers and young fruit from frost.

Grafting loquats

Usually grafted Named loquats are grafted onto loquat seedlings.

What it joins with

Loquat seedlings, and quince for a smaller tree.

When to graft

Late winter to spring.

Scion wood

This is an evergreen, so there is no dormant wood. Cut firm, recently matured tip shoots with plump buds, trim the leaves to stubs and graft within a day or two.

Best methods

  1. Cleft: Onto seedlings.
  2. Side veneer: On young potted seedlings.
  3. Chip bud: In warm weather.

Propagating loquats

Loquats sprout easily from seed; named varieties are grafted.

From seed

No. Seedlings vary and can take years to fruit.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Cleft: Onto seedlings in spring.
  2. Chip bud: In warm weather.
  3. Seed: Sow fresh; no cold needed.
