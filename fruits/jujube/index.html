🟤 Jujubes

Ziziphus jujuba

How to grow jujubes at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Most jujubes set more fruit with a second variety nearby.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 70 varieties

How to grow jujubes

Sun

Full sun and summer heat.

Soil

Any well-drained soil, pH 6.0–8.0; tolerates drought and salt.

Spacing

12–20 ft (3.5–6 m).

Planting

Plant in spring. Leafs out very late; don't give up on it.

Care

Drought tolerant once established. Remove root suckers below the graft.

Pruning

Prune in late winter; thin crowded branches.

Harvest

Late summer to autumn, when fruit turns mostly brown for crisp eating, or fully brown and wrinkled for drying.

First crop

1–3 years.

Common problems

Very few pests; suckers from rootstock.

Through the year

Spring

  • Be patient; leaves come late.

Summer

  • Remove suckers.

Autumn

  • Harvest crisp or dried.

Winter

  • Prune.

Rootstocks for jujubes

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted jujube tree. 3 options:

RootstockSizeZones
Sour jujube (Ziziphus jujuba var. spinosa) seedlingVigorous5–9
Jujube seedlingVigorous6–9
Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit

Grafting jujubes

Usually grafted Named jujubes are grafted onto sour jujube or jujube seedlings.

What it joins with

Sour jujube (Ziziphus jujuba var. spinosa) and jujube seedlings or root suckers.

When to graft

Spring, as the stock's buds begin to swell.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: In spring.
  2. Cleft: In spring.
  3. Bark (rind): For thicker stocks.

Propagating jujubes

Named jujubes are usually grafted onto sour jujube rootstock.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Whip and tongue: In spring as the stock's buds start to swell.
  2. Cleft: In spring.
  3. Suckers and division: Only from own-root trees; suckers from a grafted tree are sour jujube rootstock.
