🔴 Currants

Ribes spp.

How to grow currants at home: hardiness zones 3–7. Currants are mostly self-fertile; two varieties raise yields.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 351 varieties

How to grow currants

Sun

Full sun to part shade; likes cool summers.

Soil

Moist, rich soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

3–5 ft (1–1.5 m).

Planting

Plant in autumn or early spring a little deeper than before.

Care

Mulch to keep roots cool. Water in dry spells.

Pruning

Winter: keep 6–8 stems and remove the oldest (over 3 years).

Harvest

Early to mid summer; pick whole strings.

First crop

1–2 years.

Common problems

Currant aphid, sawfly, white pine blister rust (some areas restrict black currants).

Through the year

Spring

  • Watch for sawfly larvae.

Summer

  • Net; harvest.

Autumn

  • Plant new bushes.

Winter

  • Prune old stems.

Rootstocks for currants

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted currant tree. 2 options:

RootstockSizeZones
Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit
Golden currant (Ribes aureum), for tree formVigorous3–7

Grafting currants

Rarely grafted Currants root so easily that they are rarely grafted. Grafting onto golden currant (Ribes aureum) makes a small "standard" tree on a single stem.

What it joins with

Golden currant (Ribes aureum) and other Ribes.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto a tall golden currant stem for a standard.
  2. Cleft: Onto a golden currant stem.

Propagating currants

Currants root very easily.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Hardwood cuttings: Take 8–10 in (20–25 cm) cuttings in fall; no hormone needed.
  2. Mound (stool) layering: Also works well.

Tip: For red and white currants, rub off the lower buds so the bush grows on a short trunk.
