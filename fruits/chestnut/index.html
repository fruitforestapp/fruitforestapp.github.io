🌰 Chestnuts

Castanea mollissima and hybrids

How to grow chestnuts at home: hardiness zones 4–8. Chestnuts need two different trees to cross-pollinate.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 213 varieties

How to grow chestnuts

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Acid, well-drained, sandy soil, pH 5.5–6.5.

Spacing

30–40 ft (9–12 m).

Planting

Plant in spring; protect from deer.

Care

Water young trees. Hates wet and alkaline soil.

Pruning

Train to a central leader while young; little pruning after.

Harvest

Autumn, when burs split; gather daily.

First crop

3–5 years.

Common problems

Chestnut blight (choose Chinese or resistant hybrids), weevils.

Through the year

Spring

  • Plant two different trees.

Summer

  • Water young trees.

Autumn

  • Gather nuts daily; refrigerate.

Winter

  • Nothing needed.

Grafting chestnuts

Sometimes grafted Chestnuts can be grafted, but grafts sometimes fail years later because the variety and stock don't match.

What it joins with

Seedlings of the same species or close relatives. Chinese varieties onto Chinese seedlings; European onto European.

When to graft

Spring, once the stock is leafing out and it is warm.

Scion wood

Collect dormant scion wood in winter and keep it cold.

Best methods

  1. Bark (rind): In spring.
  2. Cleft: In spring.
  3. Chip bud: In summer.

Propagating chestnuts

Chestnuts are mostly grown from seed. Grafts often fail later because the variety and the stock don't match well.

From seed

No, but seedlings of good parents are usually good.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Seed: Never let the nuts dry out. Keep them in damp peat in the fridge over winter and sow in spring.
  2. Bark (rind): Onto seedlings of the same species, in spring.
