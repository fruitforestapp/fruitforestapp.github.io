⚫ Aronias

Aronia melanocarpa

How to grow aronias at home: hardiness zones 3–8. Aronias are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 9 varieties

How to grow aronias

Sun

Full sun to part shade.

Soil

Almost any soil, wet or dry, pH 5.0–7.0.

Spacing

4–6 ft (1.2–2 m).

Planting

Plant in spring or autumn.

Care

Very easy; few pests.

Pruning

Thin old stems every few years.

Harvest

Late summer to early autumn; very astringent raw, best in juice and baking.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Very few.

Through the year

Spring

  • Nothing needed.

Summer

  • Water young plants.

Autumn

  • Harvest; enjoy red autumn leaves.

Winter

  • Thin old stems.

Rootstocks for aronias

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted aronia tree. 2 options:

Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit

Grafting aronias

Rarely grafted Aronias grow on their own roots. They are sometimes grafted onto mountain ash (Sorbus) to make a small tree on a single stem.

What it joins with

Mountain ash (Sorbus) and sometimes hawthorn.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto a mountain ash stem.
  2. Cleft: Onto a mountain ash stem.

Propagating aronias

Aronias sucker freely and root from summer cuttings.

From seed

Fairly close. Seed needs about 90 days cold.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Suckers and division: Dig rooted shoots from the edge of the clump.
  2. Softwood cuttings: In early summer.
  3. Seed: 90 days cold.
