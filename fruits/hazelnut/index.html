🌰 Hazelnuts

Corylus avellana, C. americana

How to grow hazelnuts at home: hardiness zones 3–8. Hazelnuts are self-incompatible; plant at least two compatible varieties.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 508 varieties

How to grow hazelnuts

Sun

Full sun to light shade.

Soil

Well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

6–15 ft (2–4.5 m); as bushes or a hedge.

Planting

Plant in spring or autumn.

Care

Easy care. Remove suckers if growing as a tree.

Pruning

Thin old stems in winter for bushes; remove suckers.

Harvest

Late summer to early autumn, when husks brown and nuts drop.

First crop

3–4 years.

Common problems

Eastern filbert blight (choose resistant types), squirrels, nut weevils.

Through the year

Spring

  • Nothing needed.

Summer

  • Water young plants.

Autumn

  • Collect nuts quickly.

Winter

  • Thin stems; catkins shed pollen.

Grafting hazelnuts

Sometimes grafted Hazelnuts are usually layered, but grafting onto Turkish hazel gives a tree with no suckers.

What it joins with

Hazelnut seedlings and Turkish hazel (Corylus colurna).

When to graft

Late winter to early spring.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: In late winter.
  2. Cleft: In early spring.

Propagating hazelnuts

Hazelnuts are mostly made by layering.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Simple layering: Bend down low shoots in spring; they root by fall.
  2. Mound (stool) layering: The commercial way to make many plants.
  3. Suckers and division: Hazels sucker freely from the base.
