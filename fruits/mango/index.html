🥭 Mangoes

Mangifera indica

How to grow mangoes at home: hardiness zones 10–11. Mangoes are self-fertile.

How to grow mangoes

Sun

Full sun and heat; frost-free.

Soil

Well-drained, sandy or loamy soil, pH 5.5–7.5.

Spacing

25–30 ft (7.5–9 m); dwarf varieties 8–12 ft (2.5–3.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring or early summer.

Care

Water young trees; reduce water before flowering to encourage bloom.

Pruning

Prune after harvest to keep the tree small and open.

Harvest

Summer, 100–150 days after flowering, when the shoulders fill out and color changes.

First crop

3–5 years from grafted trees.

Common problems

Anthracnose, powdery mildew, frost.

Through the year

Spring

  • Flowering; watch for powdery mildew.

Summer

  • Harvest.

Autumn

  • Prune after harvest.

Winter

  • Protect from cold.

Grafting mangoes

Usually grafted Mangoes are grafted onto young seedlings in warm, humid weather.

What it joins with

Mango seedlings, often of tough local types such as Turpentine or 13-1.

When to graft

The warm, humid part of the year, when the stock is in a flush of growth.

Scion wood

This is an evergreen, so there is no dormant wood. Cut firm, recently matured tip shoots with plump buds, trim the leaves to stubs and graft within a day or two.

Best methods

  1. Cleft: On young seedlings.
  2. Side veneer: A common nursery method.
  3. Chip bud: In warm weather.

Tip: Many growers "pre-condition" the scion: remove its leaves a week or two before cutting so the tip bud swells.

Propagating mangoes

Mangoes are grown from seed or grafted onto seedlings in warm weather.

From seed

Some types (often Southeast Asian ones) come true from seed; many Florida and Indian types don't.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Seed: Remove the husk and sow the fresh seed; no cold needed.
  2. Cleft: Onto young seedlings in warm weather.
