⚫ Blackberries

Rubus spp.

How to grow blackberries at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Blackberries are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 54 varieties

How to grow blackberries

Sun

Full sun.

Soil

Well-drained soil, pH 5.5–7.0.

Spacing

3–6 ft (1–2 m) apart; trailing types need a trellis.

Planting

Plant in early spring.

Care

Water regularly while fruiting. Tip new canes at 3–4 ft (1–1.2 m) for erect types.

Pruning

Cut fruited canes to the ground after harvest; thin new canes to 4–6 per plant.

Harvest

Mid to late summer, when berries are dull black and soft.

First crop

1–2 years.

Common problems

Orange rust, cane blight, spotted wing drosophila.

Through the year

Spring

  • Mulch.

Summer

  • Harvest; remove fruited canes.

Autumn

  • Tie in new canes.

Winter

  • Thin and tie canes.

Grafting blackberries

Rarely grafted Blackberries aren't grafted. They are grown from tip layers, root cuttings and suckers.

What it joins with

Not grafted.

When to graft

Not grafted.

Scion wood

Not needed.

Propagating blackberries

Blackberries root where their tips touch the ground.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Tip layering: For trailing and semi-erect types, in late summer.
  2. Root cuttings: For erect types. Some thornless varieties come back thorny from root cuttings, so tip layer those instead.
  3. Suckers and division: For erect types.
