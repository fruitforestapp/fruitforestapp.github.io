🍎 Crabapples

Malus spp.

How to grow crabapples at home: hardiness zones 3–8. Crabapples cross-pollinate apples and each other.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 45 varieties

How to grow crabapples

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Most well-drained soils, pH 5.5–7.5.

Spacing

10–20 ft (3–6 m) apart, or among apple trees as a pollinizer.

Planting

Plant bare-root in early spring or potted trees any time the ground is workable.

Care

Very easy care once established. Water in dry spells for the first two years.

Pruning

Prune lightly in late winter; remove suckers and water sprouts.

Harvest

Late summer to autumn; many hang on the tree into winter.

First crop

2–4 years.

Common problems

Scab and fire blight on susceptible varieties; choose resistant ones.

Through the year

Spring

  • Enjoy the bloom; it pollinates nearby apples.

Summer

  • Remove suckers from the base.

Autumn

  • Pick for jelly when colored.

Winter

  • Prune lightly.

Grafting crabapples

Usually grafted Crabapples graft exactly like apples, and a crabapple branch on an apple tree makes a good pollinator.

What it joins with

Apple rootstocks, apple seedlings and other crabapples.

When to graft

Late winter to early spring for bench grafts; summer for budding.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto apple rootstocks.
  2. Cleft: To add a crabapple limb to an apple tree for pollination.
  3. Chip bud: In summer.

Propagating crabapples

Named crabapples are grafted just like apples, onto apple rootstocks or seedling crabs.

From seed

No, but crabapple seedlings make tough rootstocks.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Whip and tongue: Onto apple rootstocks in late winter.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
  3. Seed: For rootstocks: about 60–90 days cold.
