🌰 Almonds

Prunus dulcis

How to grow almonds at home: hardiness zones 7–9. Most almonds need a pollinizer; Independence and All-in-One are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 185 varieties

How to grow almonds

Sun

Full sun; warm dry summers.

Soil

Deep, well-drained soil, pH 6.0–8.0.

Spacing

15–20 ft (4.5–6 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring. Almonds bloom very early, so frost-free sites matter.

Care

Water in dry spells; feed in spring.

Pruning

Prune in late summer or dormant season to an open vase.

Harvest

Late summer to autumn, when hulls split open.

First crop

3–4 years.

Common problems

Spring frost, brown rot, navel orangeworm, squirrels.

Through the year

Spring

  • Protect early blossom from frost.

Summer

  • Water.

Autumn

  • Harvest when hulls split.

Winter

  • Prune.

Grafting almonds

Usually grafted Almonds are budded onto almond, peach or peach-almond hybrid rootstocks.

What it joins with

Almond and peach seedlings, peach-almond hybrids, and some plum rootstocks.

When to graft

Budding in summer.

Scion wood

For summer budding, cut budsticks of this season's growth on the day you bud, clip off the leaves and keep them damp and shaded.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.

Propagating almonds

Almonds are budded onto almond or peach seedlings, or peach-almond hybrid rootstocks.

From seed

No; seedlings may even have bitter nuts.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
