🍑 Nectarines

Prunus persica var. nucipersica

How to grow nectarines at home: hardiness zones 5–9. Nectarines are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 139 varieties

How to grow nectarines

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Light, very well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

15–20 ft (4.5–6 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring on a frost-free slope.

Care

Like peaches, but the smooth skin is more prone to brown rot and bruising. Thin fruit well.

Pruning

Prune each year to an open vase in late winter to early spring.

Harvest

Mid to late summer when fully colored and slightly soft.

First crop

2–4 years.

Common problems

Leaf curl, brown rot, plum curculio and oriental fruit moth.

Through the year

Spring

  • Prune; protect blossom.

Summer

  • Thin; harvest.

Autumn

  • Remove mummies.

Winter

  • Spray for leaf curl before bud swell.

Grafting nectarines

Usually grafted Nectarines are smooth-skinned peaches and graft exactly like them.

What it joins with

Peach seedlings, peach-almond hybrids and some plum rootstocks. Nectarines and peaches can share a tree.

When to graft

Budding in mid to late summer.

Scion wood

For summer budding, cut budsticks of this season's growth on the day you bud, clip off the leaves and keep them damp and shaded.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.

Propagating nectarines

Nectarines are a smooth-skinned peach and are budded the same way.

From seed

Sometimes, but seedlings may grow fuzzy peaches.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): In summer onto peach seedlings.
  2. Chip bud: In summer.
