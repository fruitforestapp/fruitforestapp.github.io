🟢 Limes

Citrus × latifolia, C. × aurantiifolia

How to grow limes at home: hardiness zones 9–11. Limes are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 70 varieties

How to grow limes

Sun

Full sun; very warm spot.

Soil

Very well-drained soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

10–15 ft (3–4.5 m); pots in cool areas.

Planting

Plant in spring; limes are the most cold-tender citrus.

Care

Feed regularly; keep evenly moist but never soggy.

Pruning

Light pruning to shape.

Harvest

Pick when full size and glossy; key limes turn yellow when fully ripe.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Frost, scale and leaf miner.

Through the year

Spring

  • Feed.

Summer

  • Water.

Autumn

  • Move indoors early.

Winter

  • Keep warm.

Grafting limes

Sometimes grafted Limes root well, but are also budded onto citrus rootstocks for vigor and disease resistance.

What it joins with

Trifoliate orange, citrange, Volkameriana, rough lemon and other citrus.

When to graft

Whenever the bark slips in warm weather.

Scion wood

Citrus is evergreen: use budwood, not dormant sticks. Cut rounded, recently hardened shoots with plump buds, clip the leaves to short stubs and use them within a few days.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The standard citrus method.
  2. Chip bud: In warm weather.

Propagating limes

Limes root well from cuttings or air layers.

From seed

Key limes often come true; others vary.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Semi-hardwood cuttings: With rooting hormone and bottom heat.
  2. Air layering: In spring or summer.
  3. T-bud (shield bud): Onto citrus rootstock.
