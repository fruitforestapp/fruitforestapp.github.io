🍓 Strawberries

Fragaria × ananassa

How to grow strawberries at home: hardiness zones 3–10. Strawberries are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 476 varieties

How to grow strawberries

Sun

Full sun.

Soil

Rich, well-drained soil, pH 5.5–6.5. Don't plant where tomatoes or potatoes grew.

Spacing

12–18 in (30–45 cm) apart in rows 3–4 ft (1–1.2 m) apart.

Planting

Plant in early spring with the crown level with the soil.

Care

Water regularly. Mulch with straw. Renew the bed every 3–4 years.

Pruning

June-bearers: remove first-year flowers and renovate the bed after harvest. Day-neutrals: remove runners.

Harvest

Late spring to early summer (June-bearing) or all summer (everbearing and day-neutral).

First crop

1 year.

Common problems

Gray mold, slugs, birds, verticillium wilt.

Through the year

Spring

  • Remove winter mulch; plant new beds.

Summer

  • Harvest; renovate June-bearers.

Autumn

  • Plant runners to renew the bed.

Winter

  • Straw mulch over plants in cold areas.

Grafting strawberries

Rarely grafted Strawberries aren't grafted. New plants come from runners.

What it joins with

Not grafted.

When to graft

Not grafted.

Scion wood

Not needed.

Propagating strawberries

Strawberries make new plants on their runners.

From seed

Only alpine strawberries come true from seed.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Runners: Root the first plantlet on each runner.
  2. Suckers and division: Divide the crowns of everbearing and alpine types, which make few runners.

Tip: Make new beds from young plants every 3–4 years; old plants crop less and gather disease.
