🍊 Oranges

Citrus × sinensis

How to grow oranges at home: hardiness zones 9–11. Citrus are self-fertile.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 24 varieties

How to grow oranges

Sun

Full sun; shelter from cold winds.

Soil

Very well-drained, slightly acid soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

12–25 ft (3.5–7.5 m); dwarf trees in pots.

Planting

Plant in spring, graft union above the soil. In cold areas grow in pots and bring indoors.

Care

Feed with citrus fertilizer 3–4 times a year. Water deeply, then let the top dry.

Pruning

Little pruning; remove suckers below the graft and dead wood.

Harvest

Late autumn to spring depending on variety; taste to check, color is not enough.

First crop

2–4 years.

Common problems

Frost, scale, aphids, citrus leaf miner and greening (HLB) where present.

Through the year

Spring

  • Feed; watch for aphids on new growth.

Summer

  • Water deeply; feed.

Autumn

  • Bring potted trees in before frost.

Winter

  • Protect from frost; harvest.

Grafting oranges

Usually grafted Oranges are budded onto citrus rootstocks chosen for cold, soil and size.

What it joins with

Trifoliate orange (Poncirus), citrange (Carrizo, C-35), sour orange, Swingle citrumelo, Flying Dragon for dwarfs, and other citrus. Different citrus can share a tree.

When to graft

Whenever the bark slips in warm weather, usually spring to early fall.

Scion wood

Citrus is evergreen: use budwood, not dormant sticks. Cut rounded, recently hardened shoots with plump buds, clip the leaves to short stubs and use them within a few days.

Best methods

  1. T-bud (shield bud): The standard citrus method; often done upside down (inverted T).
  2. Chip bud: In warm weather.
  3. Side veneer: Good on small potted rootstocks.

Tip: Keep newly budded citrus warm (above 70°F / 21°C) and out of strong sun until the bud takes.

Propagating oranges

Oranges are budded onto citrus rootstocks such as trifoliate orange, citrange or sour orange.

From seed

Often yes: many orange seeds grow copies of the mother tree, but seedlings take 5–15 years to fruit and have thorns.

Ways to make more plants

  1. T-bud (shield bud): Any time the bark slips in warm weather, usually spring to early fall.
  2. Air layering: Makes a tree on its own roots.
  3. Seed: Sow fresh; no cold needed. Good for rootstocks.
