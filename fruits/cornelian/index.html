🔴 Cornelian cherries

Cornus mas

How to grow cornelian cherries at home: hardiness zones 4–8. Plant two varieties for good fruit set.

How to grow cornelian cherries

Sun

Full sun to part shade.

Soil

Most well-drained soils, pH 5.5–7.5.

Spacing

10–15 ft (3–4.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring or autumn.

Care

Tough and pest-free. Flowers in very early spring.

Pruning

Little pruning; shape after flowering.

Harvest

Late summer, when fruit is dark red and drops.

First crop

3–5 years.

Common problems

Very few.

Through the year

Spring

  • Very early yellow blossom feeds bees.

Summer

  • Water in drought.

Autumn

  • Collect dropped fruit.

Winter

  • Nothing needed.

Grafting cornelian cherries

Sometimes grafted Named Cornelian cherries are budded or grafted onto seedlings; they also grow from cuttings.

What it joins with

Cornelian cherry (Cornus mas) seedlings.

When to graft

Budding in summer; grafts in early spring.

Scion wood

$_dormantScion $_summerBud

Best methods

  1. Chip bud: In summer.
  2. Whip and tongue: In early spring.

Propagating cornelian cherries

Cornelian cherries are grown from cuttings or layering, or named varieties are budded onto seedlings.

From seed

No, and seed is slow: it needs a warm spell then a cold one, often two years in all.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Softwood cuttings: Early summer, with rooting hormone.
  2. Simple layering: Reliable but slow.
  3. Chip bud: In summer onto seedlings.
