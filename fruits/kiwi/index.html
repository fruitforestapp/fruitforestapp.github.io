🥝 Hardy kiwis

Actinidia arguta, A. kolomikta

How to grow hardy kiwis at home: hardiness zones 3–8. Hardy kiwis are male or female (except Issai); plant one male for up to eight females.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 111 varieties

How to grow hardy kiwis

Sun

Full sun to part shade; shelter from late frost.

Soil

Well-drained soil, pH 5.5–7.0.

Spacing

10–15 ft (3–4.5 m) on a strong trellis or pergola.

Planting

Plant in spring; one male for up to 8 females (Issai is self-fertile).

Care

Water well. Vigorous vines need summer trimming.

Pruning

Prune in winter to a framework, and trim long shoots in summer.

Harvest

Late summer to autumn; pick firm and ripen indoors, or when soft on the vine.

First crop

3–5 years.

Common problems

Late frost on young shoots, cats chew the vines.

Through the year

Spring

  • Protect new shoots from frost.

Summer

  • Trim long shoots.

Autumn

  • Harvest.

Winter

  • Winter pruning.

Grafting hardy kiwis

Sometimes grafted Kiwis are usually grown from cuttings, but grafting can add a male branch to a female vine so one plant fruits on its own.

What it joins with

Other kiwis of the same or related species (hardy kiwi onto hardy kiwi or fuzzy kiwi seedlings).

When to graft

Late winter while dormant, before the sap rises; or budding in summer.

Scion wood

Cut pencil-thick one-year-old shoots in mid to late winter, while dormant. Keep them damp and cold in the fridge until you graft.

Best methods

  1. Cleft: To add a male or a new variety to an established vine.
  2. Whip and tongue: Onto seedlings.
  3. Chip bud: In summer.

Tip: Kiwis bleed heavily if cut in spring. Graft while fully dormant.

Propagating hardy kiwis

Hardy kiwis root from cuttings. Females need a male plant nearby to fruit.

From seed

No, and you won't know if a seedling is male or female for years.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Softwood cuttings: In early summer under a dome or mist.
  2. Hardwood cuttings: In winter, with rooting hormone.
  3. Simple layering: Pin a low vine to the soil in spring.
