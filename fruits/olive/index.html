🫒 Olives

Olea europaea

How to grow olives at home: hardiness zones 8–11. Many olives set more fruit with a second variety.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 273 varieties

How to grow olives

Sun

Full sun and long hot summers.

Soil

Well-drained, even poor or rocky soil, pH 6.0–8.0.

Spacing

20–30 ft (6–9 m); pots in cold areas.

Planting

Plant in spring.

Care

Drought tolerant once established. Needs some winter chill to flower.

Pruning

Prune after harvest or in late winter to an open center.

Harvest

Autumn: green olives in early autumn, black later. All must be cured before eating.

First crop

3–5 years.

Common problems

Olive knot, peacock spot, frost below 15 °F (−9 °C).

Through the year

Spring

  • Prune.

Summer

  • Water young trees.

Autumn

  • Harvest and cure.

Winter

  • Protect young trees from hard frost.

Rootstocks for olives

The rootstock sets the size, hardiness and often the disease resistance of a grafted olive tree. 4 options:

RootstockSizeZones
Own roots (cutting, layer or sucker)As the fruit
Olive seedlingVigorousAs the fruit
Wild olive (Olea europaea var. sylvestris) seedlingVigorousAs the fruit
Frantoio (Verticillium-resistant rootstock)VigorousAs the fruit

Grafting olives

Sometimes grafted Olives are mostly grown from cuttings, but grafting changes the variety of an established tree.

What it joins with

Other olives and olive seedlings.

When to graft

Spring, once the bark slips.

Scion wood

This is an evergreen, so there is no dormant wood. Cut firm, recently matured tip shoots with plump buds, trim the leaves to stubs and graft within a day or two.

Best methods

  1. Bark (rind): The usual way to top-work an olive.
  2. Cleft: In spring.
  3. Chip bud: In spring or early summer.

Propagating olives

Olives are mostly grown from cuttings.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Semi-hardwood cuttings: In summer, with rooting hormone, bottom heat and high humidity. Expect 6–10 weeks.
  2. Bark (rind): To put a named variety onto a seedling or old tree in spring.
