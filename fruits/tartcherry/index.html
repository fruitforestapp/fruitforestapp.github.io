🍒 Tart cherries

Prunus cerasus

How to grow tart cherries at home: hardiness zones 3–8. Tart cherries are self-fertile; one tree is enough.

Growing · Rootstocks · Grafting · Propagating · 192 varieties

How to grow tart cherries

Sun

Full sun: at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day.

Soil

Well-drained soil; avoid spots where water stands after rain. pH 6.0–7.0.

Spacing

8–20 ft (2.5–6 m) depending on variety; bush cherries 4–6 ft (1.2–2 m).

Planting

Plant in early spring.

Care

Hardy and easy. Net against birds. Mulch and water in dry spells.

Pruning

Prune in late winter or after harvest; they fruit on one-year wood, so keep new growth coming.

Harvest

Mid summer when fully colored and soft; fruit for pies, juice and drying.

First crop

3–4 years; bush cherries 2–3.

Common problems

Cherry leaf spot, brown rot, birds and spotted wing drosophila.

Through the year

Spring

  • Enjoy the bloom; one tree is enough.

Summer

  • Net and harvest.

Autumn

  • Rake fallen leaves (leaf spot).

Winter

  • Prune if not done after harvest.

Grafting tart cherries

Usually grafted Tart cherries are grafted like sweet cherries. Bush cherries usually grow on their own roots.

What it joins with

Mahaleb, Mazzard and Gisela rootstocks, and other cherries. Tart and sweet cherries can share a tree.

When to graft

Chip or T-budding in summer; spring grafts as the buds swell.

Scion wood

$_dormantScion $_summerBud

Best methods

  1. Chip bud: In summer.
  2. T-bud (shield bud): In summer.
  3. Whip and tongue: In early spring.

Propagating tart cherries

Tart cherries are budded like sweet cherries. Bush cherries and other tart cherries on their own roots also grow from suckers and cuttings.

From seed

Fairly close for some, but not reliably.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Chip bud: In summer onto Mahaleb, Mazzard or Gisela.
  2. Suckers and division: Only from own-root plants, such as most bush cherries.
  3. Softwood cuttings: Bush cherries root well from early-summer cuttings with rooting hormone.
