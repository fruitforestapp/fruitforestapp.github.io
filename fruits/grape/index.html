🍇 Grapes

Vitis spp.

How to grow grapes at home: hardiness zones 3–10. Most grapes are self-fertile; muscadines often need a self-fertile pollinizer.

How to grow grapes

Sun

Full sun and good air flow.

Soil

Well-drained soil, pH 5.5–7.0; deep rather than rich.

Spacing

6–8 ft (2–2.5 m) apart on a trellis or arbor.

Planting

Plant dormant vines in spring.

Care

Little fertilizer. Remove leaves around clusters for air and sun.

Pruning

Prune hard in late winter, leaving 2–4 canes or spurs; most of last year's growth is removed.

Harvest

Late summer to autumn; taste for sweetness, seeds turn brown.

First crop

2–3 years.

Common problems

Powdery and downy mildew, black rot, Japanese beetles, birds, wasps.

Through the year

Spring

  • Train new shoots.

Summer

  • Pull leaves around clusters; net.

Autumn

  • Harvest.

Winter

  • Prune hard on mild days.

Grafting grapes

Usually grafted Grapes root easily, but where phylloxera (a root aphid) or nematodes are a problem, wine grapes are grafted onto resistant rootstocks.

What it joins with

Grape rootstocks such as SO4, 101-14, 3309C and 1103P, and other grapes.

When to graft

Bench grafts in late winter; field grafts in spring once the vines bleed less; budding in early summer.

Scion wood

Cut dormant canes with 1–2 buds per piece in winter and store them cold and damp.

Best methods

  1. Whip and tongue: Bench-graft onto rootstock cuttings, then callus them warm before planting.
  2. Chip bud: Field budding onto planted rootstocks in early summer.
  3. Cleft: To change the variety of an established vine at ground level.

Propagating grapes

Grapes root easily from dormant cuttings. Where phylloxera (a root aphid) is a problem, European wine grapes are grafted onto resistant rootstock.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Hardwood cuttings: In winter, take cuttings with 3–4 buds; bury all but the top bud.
  2. Simple layering: Bury a cane in summer; it roots at the buried buds.
  3. Whip and tongue: Bench-graft onto rootstock cuttings in late winter, then callus them warm.
