⚫ Elderberries

Sambucus canadensis, S. nigra

How to grow elderberries at home: hardiness zones 3–9. Elderberries set heavier crops with two varieties.

How to grow elderberries

Sun

Full sun to part shade.

Soil

Moist soil, pH 5.5–6.5.

Spacing

6–8 ft (2–2.5 m).

Planting

Plant in spring.

Care

Water in dry spells; mulch. Raw berries, stems and leaves are toxic; cook the berries.

Pruning

Remove 3-year-old canes in late winter.

Harvest

Late summer, when clusters are dark purple.

First crop

1–2 years.

Common problems

Birds, cane borers.

Through the year

Spring

  • Flowers can be picked for cordial.

Summer

  • Water; net.

Autumn

  • Harvest whole clusters.

Winter

  • Cut out old canes.

Grafting elderberries

Rarely grafted Elderberries aren't grafted. They root very easily from cuttings.

What it joins with

Not grafted.

When to graft

Not grafted.

Scion wood

Not needed.

Propagating elderberries

Elderberries are about the easiest fruit to root.

From seed

No.

Ways to make more plants

  1. Hardwood cuttings: Take cuttings with two pairs of buds in winter; bury one pair.
  2. Softwood cuttings: Roots in 2–4 weeks in early summer.
  3. Suckers and division: American elderberry spreads from the roots.
