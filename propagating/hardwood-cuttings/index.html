Hardwood cuttings

WhenLate fall to late winter, while dormant
DifficultyBeginner

Step by step

  1. After the leaves drop, cut healthy one-year-old shoots about pencil thickness.
  2. Cut them into pieces 6–10 in (15–25 cm) long: a flat cut just below a bud at the bottom and a slanted cut just above a bud at the top, so you remember which end is up.
  3. Dip the bottom end in rooting hormone (easy rooters like currants, elderberries and figs don't need it).
  4. Push them two-thirds deep into a well-drained bed or a deep pot of gritty mix, with one or two buds above the soil.
  5. Keep the soil just moist through winter and spring. Most root by early summer; new leaves alone don't prove roots, so wait for steady growth.
  6. Leave them in place for the season and move the rooted plants the next fall or spring.

Fruits that grow well this way

🟡 Quinces ⚫ Mulberries 🟤 Figs 🔴 Pomegranates 🫐 Blueberries 🍇 Grapes 🔴 Currants 🟢 Gooseberries 🫐 Haskaps ⚫ Elderberries 🥝 Hardy kiwis
